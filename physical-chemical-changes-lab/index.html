Physical and Chemical Changes

1Mix and observe

New to this? Read the prelab review first: physical and chemical changes, the signs of a chemical change, kinds of mixture, and how to describe what you see.

Put two substances together and one of two things happens. Either they only mix, and each is still what it was: a physical change. Or a new substance forms: a chemical change. You cannot watch the atoms, but a chemical change often gives itself away. In this lab you put seven pairs of substances together, one pair to a test tube, and record carefully what you see before and after. A thermometer stands in for feeling a tube with your fingers. The peak temperatures were measured in the in-person laboratory on 2026-09-30; the thermometer’s time course is modelled.

Setting up the fume hood

Tip: turn your phone sideways for a bigger bench.

Empty all seven tubes and start again? What you recorded stays, but it will need checking against the new tubes.
  1. Look first. Before you mix anything, look at each chemical. Click the name in front of a bottle or jar for a close look, and record how it looks under “Before mixing” in Tables 1 and 2.
  2. Add. Click a bottle, a jar or the beaker to pick up its dropper, spatula or forceps, then click the tube it goes into. The note under the bench says which tube is next. Click anywhere else to put a tool back.
  3. The thermometer. Click it, then click a tube, to stand it in that tube. In tubes 2 and 3, put it in before the second chemical goes in, and watch it.
  4. Tube 1 is slow. Leave it alone, go on with the others, and come back to it. Do not stir it.
  5. Tubes 5, 6 and 7 are stirred. Click the glass rod, then the tube.
  6. Look closely. Click a tube for a close look at what is in it, and record it under “After mixing”.

In a real lab you would wear goggles and gloves: silver nitrate stains skin, and hydrochloric acid and sodium hydroxide burn. You would never look straight down a tube. You would feel the bottom of tube 2 with your fingers; here a thermometer does that job. At the end, tubes 1, 3 and 6 go into the waste container in the hood and the others down the sink with plenty of water.
