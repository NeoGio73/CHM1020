pH of Common Solutions

1Predict

Before you test anything, predict whether each solution is an acid, a base or neutral. Use what you know about each one. A prediction does not have to be right: it is what the experiment tests.

2Measure

Setting up the fume hood

Make your twelve predictions in part 1 and lock them. The bench opens after that, so your predictions come before your results.

Empty every well and throw away the strips? Recorded pH values will be cleared.
  1. Drag each pipette to the well with its number. A few drops go in.
  2. Drag a strip of pH paper from the vial into a well. It is dipped and pulled out at once, then laid on the paper towel.
  3. Count to 5 while the color settles. Then click the strip to hold it next to the color chart.
  4. Record the pH of the closest color, and whether your prediction was correct.

3Rank

Put the solutions in order of increasing acidity, from the least acidic at the top to the most acidic at the bottom. Use your measured pH values. Solutions with the same pH can go in either order.
