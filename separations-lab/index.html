Separations: Who Wrote the Ransom Note?

1Develop the chromatograms

New to this? Read the prelab review first: why black ink is a mixture, how chromatography pulls it apart, and how to compare the plates.

A ransom note was written with a black pen, and several black pens were found in the same room. Black ink is a mixture of dyes, and each make of pen uses its own mixture. You are the analyst: separate the ink from the note and the ink of four of the pens side by side, in two different solvents, and decide whether one of those pens wrote the note.

The colors each ink separates into were read from real strips of these pens, developed in this experiment. The development times were measured in the in-person laboratory on 2026-10-14, one plate per solvent.

Setting up the fume hood

Tip: turn your phone sideways for a bigger bench.

Clear the bench and start again with fresh plates? The times in Table 1 will be cleared.
  1. The plates. Two plates lie on the paper towel, marked Acetone and Alcohol. The ink from the ransom note is already on the middle mark, U, of each. The line and the numbers are in pencil, because ink would run.
  2. Dot the pens. Click a pen, then click each plate: the pen leaves a small dot on the pencil line, above its own number. Do this with all four pens. Click anywhere else to put a pen back.
  3. Acetone solvent. Click the acetone bottle, then the graduated cylinder: 6 mL goes in. Add distilled water the same way, up to the 8 mL line. Then click the cylinder and the chamber marked Acetone.
  4. Alcohol solvent. Make it the same way with isopropyl alcohol, for the chamber marked Alcohol.
  5. Start. Click a plate, then its own chamber, to stand it in the solvent. Record the time on the timer in Table 1. Do the same with the other plate.
  6. Watch. The solvent climbs the plate and carries the dyes with it. When it is 1 to 2 cm from the top, click the chamber to take the plate out, and record the time. The two plates are not ready together.

In a real lab you would use strips of chromatography paper standing in beakers covered with watch glasses; a plate in a glass chamber does the same job here. You would wear goggles, keep acetone and isopropyl alcohol away from flames, and leave the covers on so the solvent does not evaporate. At the end the solvents go down the sink with plenty of water, and you wash your hands.

3Which pen wrote the note?

Compare the unknown with each pen in turn, in both solvents. Two inks that are the same separate into the same colors, at the same heights, in both solvents. Inks that look alike in one solvent may still come apart differently in the other. It is possible that none of these four pens wrote the note.
